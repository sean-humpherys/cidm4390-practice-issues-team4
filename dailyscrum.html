What is Daily Scrum, who participates, and what is the objective? What is not Daily Scrum?

The Daily Scrum is a short, 15-minute event held every working day of a sprint, ideally at the same time and place to keep things simple. Its objective is to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adjust the Sprint Backlog as needed, so the team can plan the next 24 hours of work. It improves communication, surfaces impediments early, speeds up decision-making, and reduces the need for other meetings.

The Daily Scrum is for the Developers, the people doing the work of the sprint. The Product Owner and Scrum Master take part as Developers only if they’re actively working on Sprint Backlog items. The Scrum Master makes sure the meeting happens and stays within its timebox, but the Developers own it and can choose whatever structure works for them. The classic three questions (what I did yesterday, what I’ll do today, what’s blocking me) are optional, not required.

The Daily Scrum is not a status report to a manager or the Product Owner, and it isn’t a problem-solving or design session. Detailed discussions should happen right afterward with only the people who need to be there. It also isn’t the only time the team can adapt its plan, since Developers can adjust throughout the day. Finally, it isn’t a meeting that should run long or grow to include outside stakeholders who aren’t doing the sprint’s work.
